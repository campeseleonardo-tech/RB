The evidence base. Seven public event calendars were merged into one list of 1,688 distinct events over three years; duplicates across calendars were matched by country, date and name. Where several calendars cover the same world, their overlap tells how much each one misses (capture-recapture), which gives the estimated totals. Thirty events per world were drawn at random (fixed seed) to describe the typical event; 35 candidates per world were checked for their stated size to describe the top; the 30 most followed dancers per world describe individual reach. A further 162 congresses (150 at random, 12 large) were read for prices, length and line-ups. Twenty-four cities were ranked: the 15 project cities (Salzburg, Innsbruck, Vienna, Graz, Munich, Berlin, Zurich, Milan, Rome, Barcelona, Madrid, Paris, London, Amsterdam, Warsaw) and every city with at least eight Latin events. About 3,400 data rows, each with source and date. Statistics: bootstrap intervals and permutation tests. Everything is public; no logins were used.
