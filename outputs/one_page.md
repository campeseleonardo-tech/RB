# Can Red Bull enter Latin dance? (Dance Scene Atlas, one page)

**The problem.** Red Bull is strong in breaking, a spectator market. Salsa and bachata are the opposite: many people dance, pay and travel, and almost nobody watches the competitions. Red Bull has no visible presence there. Can it enter, how, and where?

**Two event markets of a different shape** (random samples of 30 events per world from the European calendars of the next 12 months, plus 20 flagship events each; Red Bull excluded).
- Breaking: a few global brands on top (Summer Dance Forever 283,000 Instagram followers, IBE 10,000 visitors) and a long tail of small one-day battles: typical event 1,641 followers, ticket about EUR 9, entry fee about EUR 10 (1).
- Latin: a broad middle of multi-day congresses paid by the dancers: typical event 4,461 followers (more than breaking, p = 0.02), 3 days, full pass about EUR 115 (up to 245); flagships with 1,000-5,000 dancers (2).
- Dancers' reach is equal at the top: the 30 most followed dancers have 4.8 million Instagram followers (Latin) and 4.1 million (breaking) (3).
- Competitions are small in both worlds: 46-125 competitors per national final (4).

**How sure are we.** Sound: Red Bull's breaking media dwarf Latin channels; Latin events are longer, paid by dancers and, typically, more followed; competitions are small in both worlds. Caveats: the two calendars come from different platforms; event sizes and sponsors are rarely published; participation in classes is measured only in two cities. Talks with organisers are the next step.

**How Red Bull could enter.** Product presence across the many mid-size and large congresses (night bar, energy for night-long dancing, sampling), as Red Bull already does at fitness and dance zones and with F45 studios (5). Not a new competition, and not sponsored dancers: their reach shows where attention is, it is not a channel to buy. Sponsors at congresses: partner pages are rarely published; the only drinks brand found is Freixenet (Croatia); Red Bull appears at none (6).

**Where.** Paris, Barcelona, Madrid (top 3 of 15 cities): a Red Bull office or dance event to attach to, a strong Latin country, a congress or championship nearby (7). Paris stays first even without country-level data; Amsterdam and London rise on city facts alone. A first, cheap pilot could run near Red Bull's headquarters (Austrian congresses).

**Next step (Leonardo).** One pilot: one congress activation, agreed with an organiser, measured (cans, contacts, dancers' feedback), before any larger plan.

**Method.** Public sources only, 15 cities, 9 countries, data in data/*.csv; every number is traced in outputs/analysis.md. Instagram counts are rounded by the platform; several figures are organiser statements.
