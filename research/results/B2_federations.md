## Notes on salsa/bachata (governance gap)

- **Salsa and bachata are social/party dances, not competitive dancesport.** They are organised through festivals, congresses, and community groups rather than national federations. No public data exists on membership numbers for salsa/bachata federations because such federations do not exist in the pilot countries (AT, DE, IT, ES, FR, NL). 
- **WDSF governs dancesport disciplines** (Standard, Latin, Breaking, Hip Hop) but does not cover social salsa or bachata as competitive categories. WDSF's discipline list includes "Caribbean Dances" which may overlap with salsa rhythms in a dancesport context, but social salsa/bachata dancing outside competition is not governed by any federation.
- **IDO includes Salsa and Argentine Tango** among its disciplines, but this is in the context of performing arts and show dance, not social dance governance. IDO has over 90 member nations but its focus is on competitive and show categories.
- **Implication for counting:** Any attempt to count salsa/bachata participants through federation data will fail because the governance structures simply do not exist for these dance forms. The scene is counted through event sizes (congresses/festivals) rather than membership rolls. This governance gap is expected and fundamental to understanding the dance scene atlas: social dances operate outside formal federation systems.

## Summary (max 200 words)

The WDSF (World DanceSport Federation) is the primary international governing body for dancesport, including Breaking (since its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 and confirmed for Dakar 2026 Youth Olympics and World Games 2029 in Karlsruhe). It has 100 member national federations across 5 continents, with 50 in Europe. WDSF recognises Standard, Latin, Breaking, Caribbean Dances, Hip Hop, and 6 other disciplines. National federations in the pilot countries (Austria's ÖTSV, Germany's DTV, Italy's FIDS/Coni-recognised federation, Spain's FEBD, France's FFDanse) all cover dancesport disciplines including Breaking but none cover social salsa or bachata. The IDO (International Dance Organization) has over 90 member nations and includes Salsa and Argentine Tango among its performing arts disciplines, but again these are show/competition categories, not social dance governance. Salsa and bachata social scenes are explicitly not governed by any federation in any of the pilot countries; they are organised through festivals, congresses, and community groups. This governance gap means that counting salsa/bachata participants requires event-based data (congress sizes, attendance) rather than federation membership rolls. All national federation membership numbers are publicly available through WDSF member pages or CONI/NOC*NSF registers, but breaking-specific athlete counts are partial (e.g., DTV lists 17 Breaking athletes, ÖTSV lists 6). Confidence is H where WDSF member data is directly observed, M where derived from organisational statements.
