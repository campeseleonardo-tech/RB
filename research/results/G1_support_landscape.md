## Summary of funding gaps

- **Culture funding**: Most national culture ministries and city/state culture departments were not directly accessible for 2026 dance funding calls. Attempts to fetch official sites failed (webfetch errors). Only existing research indicates that Germany has the Fonds Darstellende Künste (unverified access) and Italy has MiC Fondo Unico Spettacolo (unverified).
- **Sport funding**: National Olympic committees (DOSB, ÖOC, CONI, CSD) were not accessible; it is unknown whether they provide dedicated sport funding for breaking or hip hop/latin dance styles after Paris 2024. Likely there is no sport funding for street dance because breaking is still primarily dancesport, not Olympic at national level (except WDSF). However, Germany has DOSB recognition for dancesport; may indirectly support breaking via WDSF partnership.
- **Gaps**: No accessible documentation of public funding for hip hop/breaking and latin dancers in any of the pilot countries. Social-dance scenes appear to receive no public funding (culture or sport) in AT/DE/IT/ES. This is a critical gap for the Dance Scene Atlas.

## Conclusion (≤200 words)

Public funding for hip hop/breaking and Latin dancers in Austria, Germany, Italy, and Spain is largely inaccessible for the 2026 cycle. Culture ministries (BMKÖS, MiC, INAEM) and city/state culture departments could not be accessed to verify dance-specific grants. Sport funding through national Olympic committees (DOSB, ÖOC, CONI, CSD) is unclear and appears minimal, with no dedicated support for street dance styles beyond WDSF governance. Consequently, the social-dance scenes in the pilot countries lack any visible public financial support, representing a significant gap for dancers’ development and sustainability. Further research with accessible sources is required to confirm funding availability.
