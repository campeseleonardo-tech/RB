## Summary (≤200 words)
Breaking made its Olympic debut at Paris 2024 with Japan and Canada taking gold in men's and women's events. The sport was excluded from LA 2028 when the host city selected baseball/softball, cricket, flag football, lacrosse, and squash instead, prioritizing cost reduction and venue simplicity. WDSF has officially submitted a proposal for breaking's return at Brisbane 2032, citing the sport's alignment with IOC's "Fit for the Future" framework through its urban venue concept and youth appeal. The Olympic exposure has generated measurable effects: increased funding visibility for breaking federations, new brand sponsorships (Samsung supporting US breaking team), and enhanced athlete career opportunities. Despite LA28 exclusion, the WDSF maintains strong IOC relationships and is campaigning aggressively for Brisbane 2032 inclusion. The Olympic pathway appears sustainable with breaking securing Youth Olympic Games status at Dakar 2026 as a qualifying opportunity. The sport's Olympic journey demonstrates both the challenges of maintaining Olympic sport status between Games and the growing global recognition of breaking as a legitimate athletic discipline.
