### Summary (≤200 words)
Red Bull BC One began in 2004 as a 16‑person invitational in Biel, Switzerland, releasing official DVDs that soon faced music‑licensing issues. From 2006 onward the competition dropped DVDs and moved video online for free, a shift that coincided with the launch of Red Bull TV and later YouTube streaming, expanding global reach. The qualifier circuit grew from 10 countries in 2007 to 27 countries in 2025 (including Uzbekistan), with 2026 showing ~28 tour stops across six continents. In 2017 the first B‑Girl final was added, and by 2024 the format moved from six regional finals to “camps” in six host nations, reflecting a strategy of globalisation and diversification. Since 2014 Red Bull has partnered with the World B‑Boy Series to create the Undisputed solo championship, further extending the brand’s breaking ecosystem. The overall growth story is one of expanding geographic participation, digital‑first distribution, and incremental format evolution aimed at sustaining the competition’s prestige while widening its audience.

### Summary (≤200 words)
The Red Bull Music Academy was launched in 1998 as a 21‑year‑long, globally‑rotting residency that brought together emerging and established artists for workshops, lectures and performances in over 60 countries. Its associated Red Bull Radio (started 2005) reached ~600 000 monthly listeners and was syndicated to >30 countries. In April 2019 Red Bull announced it would end the RBMA and Red Bull Radio after parting ways with the creative consultancy Yadastar, citing a desire to move from a “strongly centralised approach” to a decentralized model that “empowers existing Red Bull country teams and utilises local expertise.” The shutdown took place on 31 Oct 2019; over 500 lectures were archived online. What replaced it was not a single RBMA‑style programme but a suite of more localized initiatives: Red Bull Studios (recording spaces opened from 2003 onward), the Red Bull Music Festival (still running in New York and Tokyo), and continued artist‑development activity under the Red Bull Music arm. The RBMA case is thus a cautionary example of a big‑brand cultural programme ending when its central partnership model becomes unsustainable, prompting a strategic retreat to decentralized, lower‑overhead formats.

### Summary (≤200 words)
Red Bull BC One exemplifies a brand‑led scene that grew from a 2004 invitational to a 27‑country qualifier circuit (2025) with a parallel digital‑first media strategy (VHS/DVD → free online → Red Bull TV/YouTube). The competition added a B‑Girl final (2017), shifted from six regional finals to global “camps” (2024), and partnered with the World B‑Boy Series to create the Undisputed solo champion, illustrating a strategy of geographic expansion and format diversification. The Red Bull Music Academy, founded 1998 and closed in Oct 2019 after a 20‑year partnership ended with consultancy Yadastar, stands as a cautionary case: a once‑centralised, globally‑rotting residential programme was replaced by decentralized studios, festivals and the Red Bull Music arm, highlighting the risk when a brand pivots away from a high‑cost, centrally‑managed model. Brand‑led examples beyond dance—Snipes Beyond Doubt (2024, €100 k 2 vs 2 All‑Style battle) and Nike’s AI‑driven “Never Done Evolving” tennis showcase—offer lessons: high‑value prizes and community‑driven qualifiers boost participation; technology‑enhanced storytelling can amplify reach; and format innovation (as Red Bull does across kite‑boarding, MTB and esports) must be financially sustainable, lest the programme contract when brand priorities shift. For scouting and programme design, the key take‑aways are: entry via existing community events reduces cost and risk; longevity depends on sustained brand investment; and what happens after a programme end often means a retreat to more localized, lower‑overhead formats.
