## Summary (≤200 words)

Documented gaps are real but unevenly covered by country. The EuroFIA handbook (22 union countries) confirms continuing training is self-financed and career-transition schemes are weak; the EU Lifelong Learning Programme is mentioned but dancer participation unproven. The Creative Pulse survey shows 42% freelance / 26% self-employed. Mental health: a 2023 scoping review (104 citations) documents anxiety, depression, eating difficulties, burnout and low energy; a 2024 systematic review found only one case study (N=1) of psychological intervention, confirming a large evidence and service gap. Dutch post-COVID analysis shows 80% of live-performance freelancers have unpredictable income and 30% fear job loss within 6 months. Space, government-commissioned studies, and business-skills training were not found for AT/DE/IT/ES/FR/NL in this session. Germany's KSK provides a partial social-protection framework but excludes students/employees above income limits. The core missing data: no representative national survey of dancer working conditions by country; no public dataset on studio rental prices or space availability; no government report specifically on dance workforce (Bundestag, Austrian, Italian, Spanish equivalents not surfaced). Recommended next step: request national dance association position papers directly (e.g., Tanz Deutschland, Austrian Dance Council equivalents) and search national statistics portals for NACE 85.52 company data as a space proxy.
