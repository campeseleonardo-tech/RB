# Research brief: Dance Scene Atlas for a Red Bull open application

Prepared 2026-10-19. Hand this whole file to the research model. Language of all outputs: English.

## 1. Context (read first)
A candidate for a Product/Project Manager role is building a desk-research project to show to Red Bull. The project maps dance scenes per discipline and per city, estimates how strong each scene is, compares this with Red Bull's footprint, and looks at the support athletes and teachers lack (spaces, services, income, career paths). Red Bull's presence is ONE input, not the centre.

- Pilot disciplines: (1) hip hop family, including breaking (Red Bull is already active: reference case); (2) Caribbean/Latin dance, mainly salsa and bachata and closely related dances.
- Candidate cities (can change): core Salzburg, Innsbruck, Vienna, Munich, Milan, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Berlin; reserve Graz, Madrid, Paris; benchmark cities outside Europe: New York, Cali.
- Taxonomy used: family > discipline > sub-style.
- Signals per discipline and city: community, schools and classes, spaces and venues, events, competitions, athletes, online interest, ladder of progression (class, social/jam, local battle, national competition, international competition), prices.

## 2. Rules
1. Public sources only. Do not bypass paywalls, logins or terms of service. Do not scrape platforms that forbid it.
2. Never invent a number or a source. If something is not found, say "not found", list what you searched and where.
3. Label every figure: stated (the source says it), estimated (you calculated it, show how) or inferred (a deduction, say why).
4. Give ranges where precision is not possible. Add confidence H/M/L.
5. Give the URL and the access date for every item.
6. Search in English, German, Italian, Spanish and French where relevant, and in the local language of each country.
7. No personal data of private individuals. Organisations and public professional figures (competitors with published results, Red Bull athletes) are allowed; no contact details, no social profiles.
8. Flag contradictions between sources. Do not average them silently.
9. Examples named in this brief are leads to check, not facts.

## 3. Output format (per task)
1. A table with columns: item | value | unit | status (stated/estimated/inferred) | source URL | access date | confidence | notes.
2. A summary of at most 200 words: what is known, what is missing, recommended next step.
3. For tasks tagged [METHOD]: a feasibility verdict (Yes / Partly / No) for doing this at city level from public sources, with the reason, time needed and cost.
Name each output with the task ID (e.g. A1_redbull_event_inventory).

Priority: P1 = needed before first contact with Red Bull; P2 = needed for a full MVP; P3 = later or extension.

## 4. Research tasks

### A. Red Bull footprint and strategy
- A1 [P1] Inventory of all Red Bull dance-related event series and their 2024-2026 editions: series, discipline, level (cypher/qualifier/national/world), city, country, date, venue, entry route, stated participant or audience numbers. Start with BC One and Dance Your Style; look for any other dance formats. An existing 2026 sample is in the workbook (sheet RedBull); extend and correct it. Sources: redbull.com series pages (international and country sites AT, DE, IT, ES, FR), news.
- A2 [P1] History 2015-2025 per city for BC One and Dance Your Style: where qualifiers and finals took place each year; cities where Red Bull entered, left or recurs; participant numbers where public.
- A3 [P1] Red Bull and Caribbean/Latin dance: any events, athletes, content or partnerships (salsa, bachata, dancehall, kizomba, Latin dancesport). If none, confirm the absence with a search log.
- A4 [P1] Entry route: how a dancer from a small scene reaches a Red Bull stage in each pilot country (open cyphers, invitations, rankings, national qualifiers). Documented rules only.
- A5 [P2] Red Bull dance athletes (public pages): discipline, nationality, how they entered (e.g. qualifier winner), career stories. Professionals only.
- A6 [P2] Content footprint: Red Bull Media House dance content volume and performance (videos, views, subscribers on YouTube/Red Bull TV/Instagram) compared with other disciplines.
- A7 [P2] Red Bull's public statements on events, athletes and talent strategy: interviews, press, communications. Include any "next generation" scouting programmes (a June 2026 Malaysia announcement was seen as a headline only: find the details).

### B. Ecosystem, competitors, federations
- B1 [P1] Major non-Red Bull events in hip hop/breaking and Caribbean/Latin dance in Europe: name, city, size (public), frequency, organiser type, sponsors. Leads to check: Juste Debout, Battle of the Year, IBE, Hip Hop International; for Latin dance identify the biggest salsa/bachata congresses and festivals. Flag where Red Bull is absent.
- B2 [P1] Federations and governance: WDSF (breaking, Latin dancesport), IDO, and national federations in AT, DE, IT, ES, FR, NL: recognised disciplines, membership numbers, rankings, calendars.
- B3 [P2] Breaking in the Olympics: Paris 2024 outcome, LA 2028 exclusion and its stated reason, outlook for later Games, effects on funding and sponsorship.
- B4 [P2] Other brands active in street dance and Latin dance (sportswear, drinks, media): what they sponsor and how this compares with Red Bull.
- B5 [P3] Where scenes organise online (Meetup, Instagram, Facebook groups, messaging groups, local calendars) by discipline and country, and how to count activity without personal data.

### C. Taxonomy
- C1 [P1] Hip hop family: breaking, hip hop freestyle, house, popping, locking, krump, waacking, vogue and others (confirm). Definitions, origins, competition formats, judging criteria, names in EN/DE/IT/ES.
- C2 [P1] Caribbean/Latin social dances: salsa (Cuban/casino, LA, New York/on2, Puerto Rican, Colombian/Cali), bachata (Dominican, sensual/moderna), merengue, mambo, cha-cha, dancehall; boundary cases (kizomba, zouk, Latin dancesport). Origins, spread in Europe, typical scene structure (socials, congresses, schools). Recommend which to treat as separate disciplines and which as sub-styles.
- C3 [P2] Participation segments: social, competitive, performance, fitness. Which segment dominates each discipline in Europe and what this means for Red Bull fit.

### D. Demand sizing
- D1 [P1][METHOD] National and EU statistics on dance participation (Eurobarometer sport/physical activity, Statistik Austria, Destatis, ISTAT, INE, INSEE, CBS, cultural participation surveys): share who dance, by age, sex and region if available, with survey year and definition.
- D2 [P1] Membership numbers: WDSF, national dancesport and breaking federations, dance teacher and dance school associations.
- D3 [P2][METHOD] Number of dance schools per country and city from trade registries, chambers of commerce or company registers (NACE 85.52 or equivalent). Compare with map-based counts for two cities as calibration.
- D4 [P1][METHOD] Online interest recipe: how to compare disciplines with Google Trends at country/region/city level; keyword lists in EN/DE/IT/ES (watch ambiguity such as "salsa" = sauce, "breaking" = news); normalisation; Wikipedia page views; public video and hashtag counts. Include a test run for Salzburg, Vienna, Milan, Barcelona, Berlin. State what is allowed and what is robust.
- D5 [P2] City statistics from official sources: population, share aged 15-34, students, tourist nights, income level; city versus metropolitan definitions (Eurostat Urban Audit, national offices) for the candidate cities.
- D6 [P3] Optional cultural-affinity proxies for Caribbean/Latin dance (for example residents by country of birth from official city statistics): assess validity and the risk of stereotyping, and recommend whether to use them at all.
- D7 [P2] Studies estimating dancers per capita or dance market size (academic, trade, market reports with a visible method).

### E. City supply mapping
- E1 [HOLD, do not run until told] Pilot for Salzburg: list dance schools, teachers and studios for hip hop and Caribbean dance, class slots per week, prices, venues and events per year from public sources. Report time spent per step, coverage and reliability. (This is the planned pilot, kept as a to-do.)
- E2 [P1][METHOD] Compare enumeration methods: Google Maps/Places (API cost and terms), OpenStreetMap tags, trade registries, school websites, Meetup/Eventbrite, Instagram/Facebook (terms limits), event calendars. For each: coverage, bias towards formal versus informal scenes, cost, legality. Recommend a combination.
- E3 [P1][METHOD] Event calendars: best public sources per country (AT, DE, IT, ES, NL, FR) to count socials, jams, battles and festivals for both pilot disciplines, and how to count legally.
- E4 [P2] Competitions and progression ladders per country for both disciplines: national championships, qualifiers, rankings, entry criteria, which lead to international or Red Bull events. Output one ladder per country with rungs 1-5.
- E5 [P2][METHOD] Public results and rankings to count competitors by city or country (WDSF rankings, event result pages, battle results): can competitors be attributed to cities?
- E6 [P2] Space market: rental price per hour and availability in candidate cities (sample 5-10 studios each), platforms teachers use to find space, typical constraints (insurance, licences, noise, hours).
- E7 [P2] Regulation for teaching dance commercially or running a studio in Austria, Germany, Italy and Spain: licences, trade rules, insurance, freelance tax status, recent changes.
- E8 [P3][METHOD] Informal and street scenes (parks, squares, universities, youth centres, clubs): proxies to detect scenes not visible online, and how to validate with local informants.

### F. Value and potential
- F1 [P2] Evidence on audience profile and brand value of dance events and content; sponsorship valuation methods usable for street culture.
- F2 [P2] Event economics: budgets, revenue models (tickets, sponsorship, content) and cost drivers for battles and festivals of different sizes, using public examples.
- F3 [P3] Case studies of brand-led scene development and expansion into a new discipline or market (how BC One grew since 2004; Red Bull Music Academy history and end; other brands), with lessons for scouting and programme design.
- F4 [P3] Event-driven travel: international attendance at major dance events and congresses (organiser-stated numbers) and links with destination marketing.

### G. Athlete careers and support
- G1 [P1] Support landscape for hip hop and Latin dancers in AT, DE, IT, ES: public funding, grants, scholarships, federation programmes, residencies; which apply to dance versus "sport".
- G2 [P2] Income paths for professional dancers and instructors (teaching, performing, prizes, sponsorship, content) and published surveys on incomes and working conditions.
- G3 [P2] Published evidence on gaps for dancers and teachers (spaces, funding, training, injury care, business skills, mental health). Aggregated findings only.
- G4 [P1] Existing athlete-development programmes: Red Bull (Athlete Performance Center services, athlete selection, any "next generation" scouting, Red Bull Basement structure and outputs) and others (brands, federations, Olympic programmes): eligibility, selection, content, cost, outcomes.
- G5 [P3] Design inputs for a "Basement for athletes": structure of accelerator and mentoring programmes for creators and athletes (curriculum, duration, selection, funding) and measured results.

### H. From watching to trying (later)
- H1 [P3] Platforms to find local classes and events (Meetup, ClassPass, Eventbrite, club and activity portals) and their coverage in Salzburg and Austria; gaps.
- H2 [P3] Evidence linking sport and event viewing with participation (Olympic and event effects, content-to-action funnels), especially for dance.
- H3 [P3] Public audit of Red Bull and Servus TV digital products: features that link content to local activities, weaknesses.
- H4 [P3] Salzburg: high-level inventory of what can be done (sport, dance, outdoor) and where.

### I. Practical
- I1 [P1] Legal and ethical limits of desk research on communities and athletes (GDPR, platform terms): what can be collected and published, safeguards.
- I2 [P2][METHOD] Tools for a shareable interactive map (Google My Maps, uMap, Datawrapper, Flourish, Kepler.gl, Mapbox, a published web page): features, limits, cost, privacy of sharing, filters by discipline and signal.
- I3 [P1] Red Bull recruiting: how open applications are handled, publicly visible roles in product, project, event and athlete programmes with requirements, relevant teams and offices (Fuschl, Salzburg area, Austria, Germany, Italy, Spain).
- I4 [P2] Draft 10 questions for Red Bull contacts to validate the footprint inventory and scouting hypotheses without asking for confidential information.

### J. Analysis design
- J1 [P2][METHOD] How to combine mixed indicators into a defensible score: normalisation per capita, peer-city benchmarking, sensitivity analysis, handling missing data. Draw on cultural mapping, scene measurement and urban indicator literature and name the indicators they use.
- J2 [P2] Calibration: use a discipline where Red Bull is strong (hip hop/breaking) to define a "strong scene", and address the circularity risk (Red Bull events may have helped scenes grow). Propose alternative checks.

## 5. Suggested order

2. Review results together with the candidate, then P2.
3. P3 only if the project continues.
4. E1 stays on hold until the candidate says to run it.
