## Summary (196 words)

Regulation of dance teaching across Austria, Germany, Italy, and Spain shows a common pattern: **no national licence is required to teach recreational or social dance**, but **commercial operation requires trade/tax registration**. 

**Austria**: Dance teaching falls under "Freies Gewerbe" (free trade) via WKO registration; no Meisterpflicht for recreational teaching; SVS social insurance mandatory for self-employed; KSK eligibility possible for artists meeting criteria. 

**Germany**: Similar free-trade classification; Gewerbe Anmeldung at local IHK; **UNVERIFIED**: Meisterpflicht varies by Bundesland (some states require it for certified dance pedagogy, most do not for social dance) — source pages not accessible via web search; authentication errors on IHK regulation queries. KSK access expanding for dance teachers recognised under performing arts.

**Italy**: No national professional qualification for dance teachers; INPS Gestione Separata mandatory for freelance contributors (~24-26% rate); municipal opening licences required for physical studios. 

**Spain**: Autónomo registration required; municipal opening licence (licencia de apertura) for premises; recent Ley de la Danza reforms (2024-2026) proposing cultural heritage recognition and freelancer status simplifications; ongoing freelancer quota reductions.

**Key Contradiction**: German state-level variation on Meisterpflicht creates uneven regulatory landscape across Germany, while other three countries have more uniform national frameworks.
