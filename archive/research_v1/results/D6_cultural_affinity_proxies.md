## (c) Validity assessment: does country-of-birth predict social-dance participation?

| argument | direction | confidence | notes |
|----------|-----------|------------|-------|
| First-generation residents born in Latin America / the Caribbean plausibly carry salsa/bachata cultural familiarity and are over-represented among social-dance participants and teachers. | supports proxy | H | Directionally plausible; not quantified for pilot cities in-session. |
| Second generation (born in Europe to Latin American parents) often participate in Latin social dance but are invisible in "country of birth" statistics (counted as native-born). | weakens proxy | M | Under-counts demand; effect size unknown. |
| "Bachata sensual" and much of the European salsa/bachata boom is driven by non-Latin Europeans (locals, other migrants); demand is not ethnicity-bound. | weakens proxy | H | Major segment of social-dance scenes across Europe has no Latin American origin. |
| Tourists and short-term visitors inflate dance-floor activity but are not residents; country-of-birth resident stats miss them entirely. | weakens proxy | M | Relevant for Vienna, Salzburg, Innsbruck (tourism cities), Barcelona, Berlin, Milan, Amsterdam. |
| Country of birth ≠ dance practice; many Latin American-born residents do not dance socially, and many non-Latin residents do. | weakens proxy | H | Proxy measures ancestry/demography, not behaviour. |
| Correlation with *school/teacher supply* (Latin teachers opening schools) may be stronger than with *demand* (students on the floor). | mixed | M | Not verified in-session. |

**Overall validity:** Weak-to-moderate as a standalone demand proxy. Country-of-birth is at best an indirect, noisy signal for Latin social-dance demand.

## (d) Stereotyping / ethics risk of using ethnicity proxies

| risk | severity | confidence | notes |
|------|----------|------------|-------|
| Reducing dance demand to "share of Latin American-born residents" treats an entire region's culture as monolithic and implies dance ability/interest is innate to origin. | High | H | Stereotyping risk; conflicts with Rule 7 (neutral handling of aggregate migrant statistics, no stereotyping). |
| Publishing city rankings partly driven by ethnicity could invite misreading as "these cities are more/less Latin" and could be weaponised. | High | M | Reputational risk for a Red Bull job-application product. |
| Aggregate migrant-population statistics are sensitive data; neutral presentation is required, no per-individual inference. | Medium | H | Rule 7; public aggregate stats are lawful, but framing matters. |
| Using ethnicity as a demand proxy may be legally questionable under EU equality/data-protection norms if repurposed for commercial targeting. | Medium | M | Not legal advice; flag for review. |

**Mitigation if used:** present only alongside behavioural proxies (school counts, event listings, online interest), never as a standalone ranking input, label as "demographic context only", avoid per-nationality dance claims, add an explicit ethics caveat.

---

## Recommendation: DO NOT USE as a demand proxy; use only as contextual footnote

**Reasoning:**
1. **No usable figure retrieved:** In-session searches did not surface a Latin America / Caribbean country-of-birth share for any pilot city. Per Rule 2, no such number may be invented.
2. **Weak validity:** Country of birth misses second generations, non-Latin participants (the largest segment of the European bachata/salsa boom), tourists, and does not measure actual dance behaviour.
3. **High ethics risk:** Using an ethnicity/migrant-origin proxy as a demand driver in a published product carries stereotyping and reputational risk (Rule 7) and potential legal sensitivity.
4. **Better alternatives exist:** school-registry counts (D3), online interest (D4), event listings (B5), and informal-scene proxies (E8) measure behaviour rather than ancestry.

If the product needs a "Latin cultural context" dimension, include the aggregate foreign-born / migration-background share (already published by city statistics offices) as a *contextual footnote only*, clearly labelled as demographic context — not as a predictor of dance demand.

## Summary (≤200 words)

Vienna, Berlin, Munich, Milan, and (partly) Barcelona publish residents by country of birth or citizenship via national statistical offices / city statistics departments; Salzburg and Innsbruck exist at state level via Statistik Austria but city-level Latin America breakouts were not confirmed. Amsterdam (CBS/O+S) was not successfully accessed in-session. No specific Latin America / Caribbean country-of-birth share for any pilot city was retrieved in-session; only aggregate migration-background / citizenship figures (e.g., Berlin 829,077 foreign nationals end-2024; ~40% of Austria's foreign-born population lives in Vienna). Validity is weak: country of birth misses second generations, non-Latin participants driving the bachata/salsa boom, and tourists, and does not measure dance behaviour. Ethics risk is high (stereotyping; sensitive aggregate migrant data). Recommendation: do not use country-of-birth as a Latin dance demand proxy; use only as a labelled demographic-context footnote, and rely on behavioural proxies (schools, events, online interest) instead.
