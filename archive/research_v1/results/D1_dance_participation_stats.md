## METHOD Section

### Verdict on City-Level Estimates Feasibility

**Eurobarometer:** Not feasible - national aggregates only, no regional breakdowns provided.

**Austria:** Partly feasible - EU-SILC provides regional data for some indicators, but dance-specific data lacks regional breakdown.

**Germany:** Not feasible - No national cultural participation survey; relies on time-use data which has limited regional granularity.

**Italy:** Partly feasible - ISTAT regional breakdowns available for cultural participation, but dance-specific regional data not detailed.

**Spain:** Partly feasible - ENEC provides regional breakdowns, but dance attendance data is aggregated nationally.

**France:** Not feasible - SRCV regional breakdowns exist, but dance not separately measured.

**Netherlands:** Feasible - Vrijetijdsomnibus provides detailed regional breakdowns by municipality size categories; dance included in "podiumkunsten" category.

**Overall Assessment:** City-level estimates are only reliably possible for the Netherlands (via Vrijetijdsomnibus regional data) and Austria (limited via EU-SILC). For other countries, regional dance participation estimates would require supplementary local surveys or indirect modeling.

### Data Gaps & Quality Notes

- Most surveys measure "cultural participation" broadly, not dance-specific participation
- Definitions vary: "other physical activity" vs. "artistic activities" vs. "performing arts"
- Time periods differ: 12 months vs. 12 months vs. specific quarters
- Age ranges vary: 15+, 16+, 18+, 6+
- COVID-19 impact significant for 2021-2022 data
- No direct comparisons possible between countries due to methodological differences

## Summary (194 words)

European dance participation statistics from 2022 show significant variation across countries. **CORRECTION APPLIED**: The Special Eurobarometer 525 reports that 36% of Europeans engaged in "other physical activity" (which includes dancing, cycling, gardening and other recreational/non-sport physical activity) in 2022, unchanged from 2017. This is NOT a dance-specific figure; no separate "dance" category was reported in EB 525. National data reveals the highest "other physical activity" rates in Austria (48%) and Netherlands (12%), with Southern European countries (Italy 13%, Spain 4%) showing lower engagement. France data is not available due to survey non-response issues. Time-use studies indicate Austrians spend 24 hours weekly on culture (including TV), Germans 9 hours 10 minutes, while Dutch spend 24 hours on culture plus 15 hours on entertainment media. Active dance practice is highest in the Netherlands (64% cultural hobbyists), followed by Austria (6.2% artistic activities) and Italy (2.6% active dance practice). However, most statistics measure broad cultural participation rather than dance specifically, with definitions varying widely between countries. City-level estimates are only feasible for the Netherlands (detailed regional Vrijetijdensomnibus data) and Austria (limited EU-SILC regional data), while other countries lack either dance-specific questions or regional breakdowns. Data collection methods vary significantly, and COVID-19 impacts the 2021-2022 period, complicating cross-national comparisons.
