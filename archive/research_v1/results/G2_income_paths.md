## Summary (≤200 words)

Published European dancer income data is scarce and mostly proxy-based. Germany's Künstlersozialkasse (KSK) reports an average annual income of €21,336 across all insured artists (2026), with music professionals expecting €14,191 (2022). The KSK entry threshold is €3,900/year. No dance-specific KSK breakdown was found. Hourly teaching rates from salary estimators (SalaryExpert, ERI) show €17–20/hr in DE/AT, but these are modelled estimates, not surveys. The US BLS reports a median of $24.95/hr for dancers (2023), not transferable to Europe.

Working conditions: the Creative Pulse survey (Panteia/Culture Action Europe) finds 42% of European artists freelance, 26% self-employed. Dutch live performance shows 80% freelancers with 80% reporting unpredictable income (vs. 50% EU average). Dance/NYC (2023) reports dancers earn $23K/year from dance (60% of total income), 41% did unpaid work, 54% work outside dance. EuroFIA notes continuing training is self-financed, excluding low-income dancers. The 2024 European Working Conditions Survey shows 8% of EU workers find it difficult to make ends meet.

Critical gap: no published, representative survey of dancer incomes or teaching rates in AT, DE, IT, ES, FR, NL. Most figures are US, modelled estimates, or cross-sector artist surveys.
